# T01 — Cross-Product Limit and Classification

## Formal setup

Let \(s_t=(x_t,\omega_t)'\) be generated by partial sums of a stationary
innovation vector and suppose
\[
T^{-1/2}s_{\lfloor Tr\rfloor}\Rightarrow B(r)
   =(B_x(r),B_\omega(r))'
\]
in \(D[0,1]^2\), with a continuous Brownian limit. For fixed
\(\ell\geq0\), define \(z_t=x_t\omega_{t-\ell}\).

## Assumptions

- The bivariate innovation process satisfies a joint functional central limit
  theorem.
- The lag \(\ell\) is fixed as \(T\to\infty\).
- The long-run covariance may be nonsingular or singular, but conclusions
  about rank are stated separately.
- Initial conditions are \(O_p(1)\).

## Lemmas

**Fixed-lag equivalence.** Since
\(\omega_t-\omega_{t-\ell}=O_p(1)\) for fixed \(\ell\),
\[
T^{-1/2}\omega_{\lfloor Tr\rfloor-\ell}
-T^{-1/2}\omega_{\lfloor Tr\rfloor}\overset{p}\longrightarrow0
\]
uniformly away from the origin.

**Polynomial continuous mapping.** Multiplication is continuous on continuous
limit paths, hence
\[
T^{-1}z_{\lfloor Tr\rfloor}
\Rightarrow B_x(r)B_\omega(r).
\]

## Derivation

The Riemann-sum map is continuous at continuous paths. Therefore,
\[
T^{-2}\sum_{t=1}^{\lfloor Tr\rfloor}z_t
=\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^{\lfloor Tr\rfloor}
 \left(T^{-1/2}x_t\right)
 \left(T^{-1/2}\omega_{t-\ell}\right)
\Rightarrow\int_0^r B_x(s)B_\omega(s)\,ds.
\]
Thus \(z_t=O_p(T)\) along the sample and its partial sum is \(O_p(T^2)\).
This is a polynomial-scaling statement.

It is not a classical \(I(2)\) statement. For the contemporaneous product,
\[
\Delta(x_t\omega_t)
=x_{t-1}\Delta\omega_t+\omega_{t-1}\Delta x_t
 +\Delta x_t\Delta\omega_t.
\]
The first two terms combine integrated levels with stationary shocks and are
not generally stationary. A second difference does not generically remove that
heterogeneous stochastic volatility. Therefore the definition
\(\Delta^2z_t\in I(0)\) does not follow from the \(O_p(T)\) scale.

## Rank conditions

No rank conclusion follows from the marginal rate alone. A nonsingular
bivariate Brownian limit is sufficient for a nondegenerate product functional,
but full rank of a regression design containing \(1,B_x,B_\omega,B_xB_\omega\)
must be checked as T02 does.

## Degenerate cases

- If \(\omega_t\) is stationary, \(z_t=O_p(T^{1/2})\) and its partial sum is
  \(O_p(T)\) under the simulated martingale design.
- If \(\omega_t-x_t\) is stationary, the two level columns share one common
  trend and the normalized four-column design becomes rank deficient.
- If \(\omega_t=x_t\), the interaction becomes a square and the two degree-one
  columns are identical.
- Correlation short of singularity changes the Brownian functional but not the
  exponents.

## Peer-reviewed evidence

Chang, Park, and Phillips (2001), DOI
[10.1111/1368-423X.00054](https://doi.org/10.1111/1368-423X.00054),
and Park and Phillips (2001), DOI
[10.1111/1468-0262.00180](https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-0262.00180),
provide the nonlinear integrated-regressor and asymptotic-homogeneity
foundation. Stypka and Wagner (2019), DOI
[10.1016/j.econlet.2018.12.033](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.econlet.2018.12.033),
supports the warning that polynomial transformations need their own
asymptotics rather than a conventional integration label.

## Simulation design

The committed run uses seed `20260723`, 10,000 replications, and
\(T\in\{100,200,500,1000\}\). It covers correlations \(0,0.5,0.9\), stationary
\(\omega_t\), cointegrated levels, and a singular common trend.

## Results

All 18 rate checks pass the ±0.15 exponent tolerance. Across nonsingular
integrated designs, the estimated interaction exponents range from 0.982 to
1.007 and the partial-sum exponents from 1.995 to 2.014. With stationary
\(\omega_t\), the corresponding estimates are 0.498 and 1.010.

## Verdict

**Resolved / qualified.** The cross-product and partial-sum limits are proved
under a joint FCLT. The note’s classical \(I(2)\) label is refuted; the correct
claim is a degree-two polynomial transform with \(O_p(T)\) sample magnitude.

## Concept-note implications

Use the functional limits and stochastic orders above in place of
“\(x_t\omega_t\sim I(2)\).” Carry the stationary and common-trend exceptions
into every estimator and testing claim.

## Remaining gaps

The result does not select an estimator, establish full-design rank, or provide
cross-product-specific test critical values.
