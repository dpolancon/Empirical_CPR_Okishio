# Theory Note T01: Functional Limit and $T^{-2}$ Scaling of the Cross-Product of $I(1)$ Processes

## 1. The Core Fallacy: Classical $I(2)$ vs. Product $I(1)$
The original econometric notes assumed that if $x_t \sim I(1)$ and $y_t \sim I(1)$, their product $z_t = x_t y_t$ behaves as a classical $I(2)$ process, implying a stochastic order of $O_p(T^{3/2})$ and requiring a $T^{-3/2}$ normalization for its partial sum. **This is mathematically false.**

*   **Classical $I(2)$:** If $\Delta^2 w_t = \epsilon_t$, then $w_t = \sum_{i=1}^t \sum_{j=1}^i \epsilon_j$. The variance grows at $O(T^3)$, meaning $w_t = O_p(T^{3/2})$.
*   **Cross-Product of $I(1)$:** If $x_t = \sum u_i$ and $y_t = \sum v_i$, then $x_t = O_p(T^{1/2})$ and $y_t = O_p(T^{1/2})$. Therefore, their product $x_t y_t = O_p(T^{1/2} \cdot T^{1/2}) = \mathbf{O_p(T)}$.

Because $x_t y_t$ scales as $O_p(T)$, it is strictly "weaker" than a classical $I(2)$ process. This discrepancy dictates the entire asymptotic framework for Cointegrating Polynomial Regressions (CPR) with interactive terms.

## 2. Formal Derivation of the Functional Limit
Let $x_t$ and $y_t$ be $I(1)$ processes generated by:
$$ x_t = x_{t-1} + u_t, \quad y_t = y_{t-1} + v_t $$
where $(u_t, v_t)'$ is a stationary, weakly dependent vector process satisfying the Functional Central Limit Theorem (FCLT):
$$ T^{-1/2} \sum_{t=1}^{\lfloor Tr \rfloor} \begin{pmatrix} u_t \\ v_t \end{pmatrix} \Rightarrow \begin{pmatrix} W_x(r) \\ W_y(r) \end{pmatrix} $$
where $W_x(r)$ and $W_y(r)$ are standard Brownian motions (potentially correlated).

We seek the weak limit of the partial sum of the product: $S_T(r) = \sum_{t=1}^{\lfloor Tr \rfloor} x_t y_t$.

### Step 2.1: Establishing the $T^{-2}$ Normalization
Since $x_t = O_p(T^{1/2})$ and $y_t = O_p(T^{1/2})$, the product $x_t y_t = O_p(T)$. 
The sum of $T$ terms of order $O_p(T)$ yields a stochastic order of $O_p(T^2)$. Therefore, to obtain a non-degenerate asymptotic distribution, we must scale the partial sum by **$T^{-2}$**.

*(Note: If we had applied the $T^{-3/2}$ scaling proposed in the original notes, the sequence $T^{-3/2} \sum x_t y_t = T^{1/2} [T^{-2} \sum x_t y_t]$ would diverge to infinity as $T \to \infty$.)*

### Step 2.2: Application of the Continuous Mapping Theorem (CMT)
We rewrite the normalized partial sum as a Riemann sum:
$$ T^{-2} \sum_{t=1}^{\lfloor Tr \rfloor} x_t y_t = \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^{\lfloor Tr \rfloor} \left( \frac{x_t}{\sqrt{T}} \right) \left( \frac{y_t}{\sqrt{T}} \right) $$

By the FCLT, the standardized processes converge weakly in the Skorokhod topology:
$$ \frac{x_{\lfloor Ts \rfloor}}{\sqrt{T}} \Rightarrow W_x(s), \quad \frac{y_{\lfloor Ts \rfloor}}{\sqrt{T}} \Rightarrow W_y(s) $$

Because the mapping $f(a, b) = a \cdot b$ is continuous, and the integration mapping $g(f) = \int_0^r f(s) ds$ is continuous for continuous limit processes, we apply the Continuous Mapping Theorem (CMT) (cf. Phillips, 1986; Park & Phillips, 1989):

$$ \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^{\lfloor Tr \rfloor} \left( \frac{x_t}{\sqrt{T}} \right) \left( \frac{y_t}{\sqrt{T}} \right) \Rightarrow \int_0^r W_x(s) W_y(s) ds $$

**Theorem 1 (Cross-Product Limit):**
> The partial sum of the product of two $I(1)$ processes, scaled by $T^{-2}$, converges weakly to the integral of the product of their respective Brownian motions:
> $$ T^{-2} \sum_{t=1}^{\lfloor Tr \rfloor} x_t y_t \Rightarrow \int_0^r W_x(s) W_y(s) ds $$

## 3. Implications for OLS Estimation Rates
This $T^{-2}$ scaling fundamentally alters the convergence rate of the OLS estimator $\hat{d}$ for the interactive term in the regression $Y_t = d(X_t Y_t) + \dots + e_t$.

Let $R_t = x_t y_t$.
1.  **Denominator ($\sum R_t^2$):** 
    Since $R_t = O_p(T)$, $R_t^2 = O_p(T^2)$. The sum over $T$ terms is $O_p(T^3)$. 
    Normalization: $T^{-3} \sum R_t^2 \Rightarrow \int_0^1 W_x(s)^2 W_y(s)^2 ds$.
2.  **Numerator ($\sum R_t e_t$):** 
    Assuming the cointegrating error $e_t \sim I(0)$, $e_t = O_p(1)$. 
    The product $R_t e_t = O_p(T)$. The sum is $O_p(T^2)$.
    Normalization: $T^{-2} \sum R_t e_t \Rightarrow \int_0^1 W_x(s) W_y(s) dW_e(s) + \Lambda_{Re}$ (endogeneity correction).

**The Convergence Rate of $\hat{d}$:**
$$ \hat{d} - d = \frac{\sum R_t e_t}{\sum R_t^2} = \frac{T^{-2} \sum R_t e_t}{T^{-3} \sum R_t^2} \cdot T^{-1} = O_p(T^{-1}) $$

**Crucial Correction:** The estimator $\hat{d}$ converges at rate **$T$** (standard super-consistency), exactly the same rate as a standard $I(1)$ cointegrating vector. It **does not** converge at $T^{3/2}$ or $T^2$. The original notes' assumption that the interaction term yields a faster convergence rate due to being "I(2)" is incorrect.

## 4. The Endogeneity Problem (Why Wagner & Hong Excluded Cross-Products)
The limit of the numerator involves the stochastic integral $\int W_x(s) W_y(s) dW_e(s)$. 

In standard FM-OLS (Phillips & Hansen, 1990), the regressor is $I(1)$, and the integral is $\int W_x dW_e$. Because $W_x$ is a martingale (or can be projected onto one), the endogeneity bias $\Lambda_{xe}$ is a constant matrix that can be estimated via Long-Run Covariance (LRCV) and subtracted.

However, **$W_x(s) W_y(s)$ is not a martingale**, nor is it a standard Brownian motion. Its quadratic variation is highly complex:
$$ d(W_x W_y) = W_x dW_y + W_y dW_x + d\langle W_x, W_y \rangle $$
Because the "regressor" in the limit theory is a product of Brownian motions, the endogeneity correction term is no longer a simple constant nuisance parameter. It is a path-dependent stochastic integral that cannot be purged using standard FM-OLS kernel estimators. 

**Conclusion for T01:** 
This mathematical reality is precisely why Wagner & Hong (2016) restricted their CPR framework to integer powers ($x_t^2, x_t^3$) where the limits remain tractable, and excluded cross-products of distinct integrated variables. Estimating $d$ consistently requires either the IM-OLS partial sum transformation (which bypasses the LRCV entirely) or a novel, custom-derived FM-OLS correction matrix specifically for the $\int W_x W_y dW_e$ topology.
